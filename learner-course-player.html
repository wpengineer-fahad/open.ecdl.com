3.2 Drafting Charge-Sheets & Show-Cause Notices under Section 24

Faculty: Advocate Rafiqul Islam • Advocate, Supreme Court of Bangladesh

Section 24 Statutory Procedural Safeguards

Under Section 24(1)(b) of the Bangladesh Labour Act 2006 (incorporating all gazette amendments up to 2026), no order of dismissal under Section 23 shall be made unless the accused worker is served with a written charge-sheet clearly specifying the alleged misconduct and is granted a statutory minimum of 7 (seven) calendar days to submit an official explanation.

Key Elements of an Irreproachable Charge-Sheet:

  • Precision of Allegation: State explicit dates, times, and witnessed actions. Ambiguous terms like "disruptive conduct" will be struck down by the Labour Appellate Tribunal.
  • Statutory Sub-Clause: Explicitly cross-reference the alleged misconduct to Section 23(4) (e.g. habitual absence, fraud, or damage to employer property).
  • Service of Notice: Under Bangladesh Labour Rules 2015 Form-19, delivery must be provable via recipient signature or registered postal acknowledgement.

Can the 7-day reply period be shortened if both management and the CBA agree in writing?

Advocate Rafiqul Islam (Faculty): No. The 7-day minimum in Section 24 is a statutory substantive right of the worker; bilateral agreements cannot contract out of mandatory statutory protections.
